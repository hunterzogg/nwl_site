Started pts is what a team's pickups scored in its lineup while it rostered them. Value is points over replacement in those starts, the same measure the site's trade grades use: each start counts only what a player scored above the 13th-18th best QB, TE or Head Coach that week (31st-36th for RB and WR).

For the price compares value with the bid. Across every paid claim, the league got 0.41 points of value per FAAB dollar, so a pickup is judged on its value minus 0.41 × its bid. A $0 pickup that started is always a good deal, and a $70 bid needs about 29 points over replacement to break even.

FAAB accuracy winning bid vs the next-highest bid

A perfect bid is $1 more than the next team was willing to pay, or $0 when nobody else put in a claim. Anything above that was money left on the table. Of the $1,100 spent on waiver claims, $471 went to bidding past the next team on 47 contested claims (the median winner paid $7 more than the next bid), and $200 went to 30 paid claims nobody else bid on. That makes the league 39% accurate overall.

Accuracy is the share of a manager's waiver dollars that it actually took to win: 1 minus (dollars over the next bid + $1, plus dollars bid when no one else claimed the player) ÷ dollars spent. The next bid is the highest valid claim on the same player in the same waiver run. ESPN also records bids that failed because the player the bidder meant to drop was already gone; those weren't valid competing bids, so they're left out. Free-agent pickups (no bid) aren't included. Ties go to waiver priority, so a $1 edge is the cheapest sure win.
